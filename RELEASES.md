# Release notes

## 0.5.0: a rule of life, a second translation and the earliest writings

Your notes for this round, one by one, then something I found that you did not ask about.

### What you asked for

- **"A short 'about' for each book."** Every book of the Bible has an introduction: what it is,
  who wrote it and when (the Church's tradition and, where it differs, what most scholars think,
  without polemics either way), its language, the world it came from, what to look for, and two to
  four passages worth knowing, shown in the words of the Bible and a tap from the reader. Open it
  from "About Genesis" under the title of chapter 1, or from the book picker. They were drafted
  from standard Catholic and scholarly reference works and then checked independently, field by
  field; every key passage was opened in the app's own text. Pinpoint references that could not be
  confirmed, Catechism paragraph numbers among them, were taken out rather than risked.
- **"Non-canonical writings from the very early, at the end of the Bible, in a drop-down."** At the
  end of the book picker, after Revelation and kept apart from the books, is a fold marked
  **Early Christian writings, Not Scripture**. It holds what you suggested and what goes with it:
  the **Didache**; **Saint Clement of Rome's** letter to the Corinthians; the seven letters of
  **Saint Ignatius of Antioch**; **Saint Polycarp's** letter to the Philippians and the
  **Martyrdom of Polycarp**; the **Letter to Diognetus**; and **Saint Justin Martyr's** First
  Apology, whose last chapters are the oldest account of the Sunday Mass. Each has a short
  introduction, and every page says "not Scripture". They are also under Pray, Library. I kept to
  works by saints or of the apostolic age that the Church has long honoured, in public-domain
  translations; Ignatius is given in the shorter form scholars accept as his own.
- **"What open source modern translation can we incorporate? (sacredbible.org)"** That site is the
  **Catholic Public Domain Version**, and it is in: choose it in the reader's text settings (the
  "Aa" button) or in Settings, Reading. I made it an option, never the default, for a reason you
  should know: it has **no imprimatur**. It is one layman's translation (Ronald L. Conte Jr.), no
  bishop has reviewed it, and its translator has been criticised for opinions of his own. The
  translation itself reads plainly and follows the Latin closely. So the Douay-Rheims stays the
  default, the app says what the CPDV is wherever it is chosen, and the prayers always quote the
  Douay-Rheims. It sits on the Douay-Rheims' verses, so your notes, highlights and links stay on
  the same verses in either; in the Song of Songs it names who is speaking, shown as small red
  rubrics. If you read the CPDV for a while and switch back, Settings, Storage offers to remove it.
- **"Prayer intentions quick button to open and edit from the daily page."** "Intentions" sits at
  the foot of the For today card and opens your intentions in a sheet: add one, change one, mark one
  answered, without leaving Today. There is also an Intentions card you can put on Today (below).
- **"Daily prayers need to feel way less like a checklist."** The card is now **For today**: what
  you try to do today from your **rule of life**, each with a small icon for what it is (a prayer,
  a devotion, the Mass, a guided prayer) and, for what does not come every day, a word saying why it
  is there today: "First Friday", "Fridays, in Lent", "Once a year". Tapping one opens it. There
  are no boxes and, by default, **no marks at all**: "Mark what I have prayed" is off unless you
  turn it on in Settings. If you had been marking in 0.4, your phone keeps them on; you can turn
  them off there. An empty rule invites you with **Help me choose**: five starting rules by state
  of life (just beginning, a busy parent, working with little time, a student, with more time),
  each a few things you can untick, as Saint Francis de Sales advises.
- **"Be careful of the pitfalls that came up during study."** The shower of symbols on finishing
  the Rosary, the Stations, a guided prayer, a novena day or a plan day is **gone**; "Well done." is
  gone. Nothing counts streaks or days missed. Nothing tells anyone what stage of the spiritual
  life they are in. Counsel is opened by the reader, never pushed. The examination of conscience
  says to keep it short, and the scruples page says plainly when to see a doctor as well as a
  confessor.
- **"Deep dive: an app for helping people become saints, in the good times and the bad."** Under
  Pray, **For times like these**: counsel from the saints for fourteen times of life. In hard
  times: dry prayer, having missed your prayer, after a fall into sin, temptation, anxiety,
  suffering, grief, feeling far from God, scruples, being too busy, and when it is all too much
  (with crisis lines to call or text from the page). In good times: joy, when things go well, and
  beginning again. Each gives the counsel in the app's own words with the saints and works it
  comes from, what to do now, Scripture in the Bible's words, and a prayer. Also new: thirty
  **short prayers** to carry through the day, one on Today; **Offering the day**, a guided morning
  prayer; **A monthly recollection** and **A yearly review**, which the rule of life can schedule;
  a word on **keeping Sunday** on Sundays and holy days; and an **Advice** tab in Confession for
  what your confessor told you, kept from one confession to the next and private like the rest.
- **"Make the dashboard customizable; the reading-plan prompt clutters it."** Today has **Arrange
  this page** at the bottom (also in Settings): show, hide and move each card. The reading-plan
  card now appears **only while you follow a plan**; until then a quiet "Reading plans" link sits
  on the Continue reading card. Cards you can add: Intentions, today's Journal, For times like
  these. "On this day" and the rest can be hidden.
- **"The journal shouldn't be so structured, but I like the examen populating it."** A journal page
  is now **free writing**, at the top. Headed parts follow it, and appear only where the examen or
  another guided prayer wrote them ("Thanks", "Where God was at work", "For tomorrow") or where you
  add one from the headings under the page. Nothing you wrote before is changed.
- **"The bottom buttons move up a little as I scroll."** That is an iPhone quirk with bars fixed to
  the bottom of a scrolling page. The page no longer scrolls; each screen scrolls inside it, which
  leaves the tab bar where it is. **I could only test this in Chromium; please check it on your
  phone** (see below).
- **"Autosave typed notes; the Save button makes it hard to tell what is saved."** Verse notes,
  reflections, the journal, your saints' notes and the confessor's advice now save themselves a
  moment after you stop typing, when you leave the field, when you close the sheet and when you
  put the app away, and a line says "Saving…" then "Saved". There is no Save button. A note sheet
  has **Done**; a new note can be **Discarded**; an existing one can be deleted, and changing your
  mind at "Delete this note?" keeps what you had just typed.

### Something you did not ask for: the Bible text had errors

Lining the CPDV up with the Douay-Rheims verse by verse showed that the text the app has shipped
since 0.1 had **lost words in transcription**, in more than two hundred verses. Some reversed the
sense: Sirach 15:8 read "Lying men **shall be** mindful of her" (it is "shall **not** be"), and
2 Corinthians 2:1 "I determined… **to come** to you again in sorrow" (it is "**not** to come").
John 17:1 had lost "Father,", 1 Peter 1:1 "Galatia", 1 Samuel 17:49 "the stone was fixed in his
forehead", 2 Timothy 2:1 "the grace which is in". Fourteen psalms had lost the "Alleluia." that
opens them. And Baruch 6:7 printed the words of verse 37.

The build now restores them from the second transcription we already compared against, on strict
conditions (it must agree on every other word of the verse, and none of its own known faults may
come with it). An independent audit then judged every one of the 261 restorations against the
Latin Vulgate, from which Challoner translated. **31 had nothing behind them in the Latin** (a word
repeated from nearby, "scale **down** the walls" for "scale the walls", a garble) and are refused
by name; 230 stand. Where the two transcriptions agree against the Latin and nothing settles it,
the old reading stays. Verse numbering is unchanged, so nothing you attached to a verse moves.

**If you saved the Bible for offline use,** the corrected books arrive by themselves the next
time the app is opened after you upload this release, without discarding anything else you saved.
That only happens once **zip 2** is on the site (see Upgrading).

### Upgrading from 0.4

Nothing to do on your device, and nothing you wrote is converted. Your daily list is your rule of
life; entries keep their days. Whether marks stay on is decided once, from whether you ever used
them. Backups from any earlier release restore into 0.5.

For a deployment from a phone, upload **zip 1** (the app, now 79 files), **zip 2** (the
Douay-Rheims, corrected), **zip 4** (the Fathers, now with the early Christian writings,
81 files) and **zip 5** (the CPDV, new, 73 files). **Zip 3 is unchanged**; skip it. Files with
the same names are replaced and none needs deleting. Then open `check.html`: it now checks the
CPDV and the early writings too. `docs/PUBLISHING.md` has the steps.

For a deployment from folders: replace the files; `data/cpdv/`, `data/early/`,
`data/introductions.json` and `data/early.json` are new.

`TEXT_VERSION` was not bumped: devices keep the Latin and the Fathers they saved, and fetch again
only the Douay-Rheims books they kept.

### What the independent reviews found

Three reviews were made before release by separate agents that had not written the work: the
code, run against its own server; the spiritual and pastoral content; and the corrections to the
text, judged against the Latin. Everything below is fixed.

**The code.** The Song of Songs speaker rubrics used a regular expression that Safari cannot read
before iOS 16.4; one such line stops the whole app on an older iPhone. A verse note was lost if
you chose "Add to a reflection instead" and then changed your mind; edits typed just before
"Delete" were lost if you cancelled; and the confessor's advice dropped what was typed since the
last pause when you changed tab. A device could record the text corrections as done while the
site still had the old text (a phone upload of zip 1 alone), and then never take them; the worker
now checks the revision inside each file. Five verses had a stray mark where a restored word had
been lost. Search could search the Douay-Rheims while saying it searched the CPDV after a failed
download. With the CPDV chosen, the prayers quoted the CPDV; they now always quote the
Douay-Rheims, and say so if a book is not yet on the device. Smaller: the chapter list of an early
writing hid the heading under the bar; screen readers heard "Saving…" at every keystroke; the
fold in the book picker showed no sign it could open.

**The content.** The temptation page said "only consent is sin" in a way that excused dwelling on
a temptation; it now says, as Saint Francis de Sales does, that sin begins with consent or with
choosing to dwell on the pleasure. The scruples page now says that severe or lasting scruples can
be an illness, obsessive-compulsive disorder, that a doctor can treat. Despair, suffering, grief
and dryness each gained a plain word on when a doctor is needed; the crisis lines now cover Canada
(988) and the United Kingdom and Ireland (116 123) and can be tapped. Justin's Apology had lost
the rescript of Hadrian that Justin himself appended; it is back, and only the later forgeries are
left out. "Biblica keeps no count of days" was not true with marks on; it now says what is true.
And a dozen smaller corrections of fact and wording, in the introductions to the early writings,
the CPDV's description and the starting rules (Sunday Mass is no longer "when I can").

**The text.** As above: 31 restorations refused, five stray marks removed, and fourteen psalm
titles found missing.

### Known limitations

- **Nothing here has run on a physical phone.** The tab-bar fix in particular depends on how
  Safari behaves on an iPhone; it was checked in Chromium.
- **With the page fixed, tapping the status bar no longer scrolls to the top** on an iPhone.
- **The CPDV has no imprimatur**, and in two places (2 Samuel 13:39 and Psalm 43:6) it runs a verse
  into the one before, so a note on that verse has no verse to sit beside in the CPDV.
- **The Douay-Rheims text is still two transcriptions collated by machine and judged against the
  Latin**, not a proofreading against the printed 1899 edition. 61 restorations are variants
  between printings that render the Latin equally well; where the transcriptions agree against
  the Latin (Philippians 4:13, "all these things"), the old reading was kept.
- **The introductions are summaries**, checked once. Where scholars disagree they say so; they
  are not the last word.
- **The early writings are in nineteenth-century English**, as the translators left them, with
  their occasional slips. Clement's letter lacks the passage recovered in 1873, including its great
  prayer for all people and for rulers, and its last chapters are numbered as in that translation.
- **The counsel is not a substitute** for a confessor, a doctor or a friend. Its crisis lines are
  for the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and Ireland; elsewhere it says to call the
  local emergency number.
- **Searching the CPDV the first time downloads every book of it** (about 5 MB), as searching the
  Douay-Rheims always has.
- **Saving both translations for offline use takes about 5 MB more**; Settings offers to remove
  the one not in use.
- Everything listed under 0.4.0 below still applies, except where this release changed it.

## 0.4.0: updates, privacy, the Mass and a list without boxes

A round of polish that turned up more than polish.

### Why your copy was not updating

I cannot see your phone, so this is a diagnosis from the code, in order of likelihood.

1. **The app only looked for a new release when it was started afresh.** A home-screen app on an
   iPhone is almost never started afresh: it is resumed from where it was. So it could go days
   without asking, and there was no button to make it ask. This is the most likely cause, and it
   was a design gap, not a mistake in your upload.
2. **An upload with one file missing was silently ignored.** A release is installed whole or not
   at all, which is right, but when a file was missing nothing said so. Uploading 56 files from a
   phone, one can be missed.
3. **GitHub Pages hands out a new upload over about ten minutes.** Opening the app straight after
   committing finds nothing new, and with cause 1 there was no second look.

The review of this release found a fourth. It is probably not what held 0.3.0 back, since that
release did change `sw.js`, but it would have caught you sooner or later:

4. **A change that left `sw.js` alone was never noticed at all.** The browser decides whether
   there is a new release by comparing `sw.js` with the copy it has. Any commit that changed other
   files but not that one never reached an installed copy, and Settings would have said the app
   was up to date.

All four are dealt with:

- The app now asks the site for a new release **whenever it comes back into view** (at most once
  in five minutes), every half hour while it stays open, and when a connection returns. If the
  site does not answer, it asks again a few times.
- **Every file of a release has a fingerprint recorded in `sw.js`.** So any change to any file
  changes `sw.js`, and the browser notices. A device installs a release only if every file it
  fetches matches its fingerprint: a file missing from the upload, or one left over from the
  release before, stops the install, the copy you have carries on, and the app looks again a
  minute later in case the upload was still going.
- A new release is **put in place by itself**: at once if you have only just opened the app,
  otherwise the next time you go to one of the five main tabs or come back to the app. It never
  restarts while you are typing, in the middle of a guided prayer, with a sheet open or while you
  are following the Mass; never twice in five minutes; and not after you have said "Later". It
  returns you to the same screen at the same place. If you would rather be asked, turn off
  "Update by itself" in Settings and you get the Refresh banner instead. After an update the app
  says so once.
- **Settings, Updates** shows the version, has **Check for updates**, and tells you plainly what
  it found: up to date; a new version arrived; a new version is published but has not reached this
  device yet; a new version is published but these files are missing or out of date on the site;
  or the device had no room for it.
- **Fetch afresh** fetches every file of the app again and checks each one, replacing the copy on
  the device only if all of them arrived and are right. It leaves your writing alone, and changes
  nothing if the site cannot be reached or is incomplete.
- A new page, **`check.html`**, is for you as publisher. Open
  `your-name.github.io/biblica/check.html` after uploading: it reads the release's own list of
  files, fingerprints each one, and names any that are missing or not from this release. It also
  checks that the Bible and study files are there.

**To get this release onto a copy that is stuck:** upload it, open `check.html` in Safari to
confirm nothing is missing, wait ten minutes, then close the app completely (swipe it away in the
app switcher) and open it. Leave it on Today for a minute. It should offer Refresh; if not, close
and open it once more. From 0.4.0 on, none of this should be needed again. Do **not** delete the
app from the home screen to force it: on an iPhone that erases everything you have written in it.

### Storage: what I checked, and why this app will not grow like Bloom

You asked me to make sure Biblica does not swell over time. I read Bloom's code as well as this
app's.

Biblica keeps three kinds of thing and nothing else:

| What | Size | Can it grow? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The app itself | 1.4 MB | No. Each release replaces the last; the old one is deleted. The update test publishes fourteen uploads in a row and checks that one release remains |
| The Bible, and the Latin, cross-references and Fathers you have opened | up to 4.7 MB and 34 MB | Only up to the size of the files on the site. They are saved once and survive updates |
| What you write | 392 bytes for an empty app; about a kilobyte for a long journal entry | Text only. A journal entry every day for ten years is a few megabytes |

There is no media, no record of what you read or where you went in the app, and no cache of
pages visited. The one record the app keeps by itself is the quiet note of what you prayed each
day, a few bytes a day, and it can be turned off. Two things were tightened: the service worker
used to keep any file of the site it was asked for, and now keeps only the release's own files,
each checked, and the per-book texts; and Settings now **shows** the figures and lets you remove
the study material, which is about 85% of the possible total.

Bloom's own storage is also bounded, but it is bigger than it needs to be: its 44 fruit pictures
are 9.6 MB of PNG, and because they are kept in the cache that is replaced at every release, every
update downloads all of them again. Converting them to WebP and keeping them in a cache of their
own would cut that to about a megabyte, fetched once. I have not changed Bloom; say if you want
that done. Beyond what either app stores, the figure iOS shows for a home-screen app includes
WebKit's own overhead, which neither app controls.

### What I held back, and why

**The full English text of the Mass.** The current translation belongs to ICEL. Its published
policy does allow approved texts on a non-commercial website without asking, on conditions: no
fee, the copyright notice on the first and last screens, the text exact, and the permission can be
withdrawn. Biblica as you publish it probably qualifies as such a site. I still gave only the
people's responses, the priest's short lines (none longer than sixteen words) and the first words
of the longer prayers, for three reasons: the policy says it grants no licence "to publish texts
in any other form", and an app that installs and works offline is arguably another form; the text
must be exact, and I will not type the Missal from memory; and you are the publisher, so the
choice to rely on that policy should be yours, made knowingly. The same doubt applies, in a small
way, to the excerpts that are there, and the terms say so. The notice ICEL asks for is on the
screen as it stands. If you want the full people's parts, the honest routes are to write to ICEL
for permission or to decide to rely on the web policy; the data file has a place for each text.

**The Divine Office.** See the section in the roadmap. In short: the current Liturgy of the Hours
is under copyright in English, psalms included, and changes daily, so the app points to the sites
that carry it with permission. What can be bundled honestly is the older Office, and the natural
first piece is Compline, built from the psalms and Latin the app already has. I did not build it
this round: the Mass text took the care I had for liturgical text, and I would rather do Compline
properly than quickly.

**The propers of the Latin Mass.** The Introit, Collect, Epistle, Gospel and the rest change every
day and follow the 1962 calendar, which the app does not yet have. The companion marks where each
comes and says it is not in the app.

  refusing an incomplete site; and at the end one release on the device, byte for byte the
  published one, with the note written at the start still there.

That script found a real fault in my first version of "Fetch afresh". It discarded the app's
files and reloaded, and the browser's own cache handed back files from **the first release ever
loaded**, two versions old. The second version was worse in a way the script did not try (see
below). It is now done by the service worker, which replaces nothing unless every file is right.

### What the independent reviews found

Five reviews were made before release by separate agents that had not written the code: the
app's code, run against a simulated host; the pipeline and the documents; the facts of the Mass
companion and the Office page; the calendar notes from 9 December to 8 February; and, after the
fixes, a second look at the update path alone. They found real faults. Everything below is fixed,
and tested where a browser can show it.

**The update path, which matters most.**

- Over an incomplete upload of a newer release, the second "Fetch afresh" wrote what files there
  were into the working copy and left an app that would not start, online or off.
- A host answering with two versions of `sw.js` by turns, as a CDN can for a few minutes after an
  upload, made the app restart itself sixteen times in forty seconds.
- A change that left `sw.js` alone was never noticed; and `check.html` called an upload complete
  when only `sw.js` was new and every other file was the release before. The fingerprints close
  both.
- The app could restart itself on the way to a screen whose Back would then be lost, after
  "Later", while the Mass was open on a phone being locked and woken, and while a note was still
  being saved. A restart between screens also put the new screen at the old one's scroll position.
- `check.html`, opened in a browser that still held 0.3.0, was answered from 0.3.0's saved files
  and reported a complete upload as broken.
- Without `.gitattributes`, a checkout on Windows could change line endings, after which every
  fingerprint would fail and no device could install anything. Added, with `.nojekyll`.

**Elsewhere in the app.** A yearly repeat could not be saved once a month limit had been chosen;
with "Mark what I have prayed" off the app still recorded it; the screen could be kept awake after
leaving the Mass; jumping to a part of the Mass hid its title under the header; the update banner
covered the Back button and could not be put off; erasing and restoring left old state in memory
until the app restarted (both now restart it); "Last backup" was wrong after a restore; the storage
figures misled in a browser without offline storage; and the focus was lost after each weekday
toggle.

**The Mass.** No posture in the ordinary form was wrong, and every response checked word for word
was right. One people's "Amen" was missing, after the prayer for peace. "Today it is not said" was
wrong at a Saturday-evening Mass, which is Sunday's. Nine statements were inexact: the Kyrie's
history, the first reading "chosen to go with the Gospel" (on Sundays), the Tract "in Lent" (from
Septuagesima), the Leonine prayers (the Sacred Heart invocation came under Pius X), three on the
Office page, and the bishops' page, which is an outline without the prayers. In the traditional
Mass the build had given the priest's Gloria Patri at the Lavabo to the server, dropped a line at
the Gospel and the end of the Secret, split the Hanc igitur and the Communicantes mid-sentence,
printed rubrics as words of the prayer, and left out the Asperges and the incensing a sung Mass
has. Postures at the Latin Mass differ from place to place; the companion gives common custom and
says so.

**The calendar notes.** In 158 entries from 9 December to 8 February nothing was on the wrong day.
Two changes and five hedges, listed above.

**The documents and the pipeline.** The privacy statement understated what the host sees and left
things out: it now says how often the app asks for updates, that books are fetched as the day's
readings and prayers need them, that session storage holds the screen across a restart, and that
whoever publishes the copy decides what code it runs. The terms and notices did not mention the
Missal excerpts; now they do. `uv run biblica-build` with no step named failed; the Markdown
converter accepted much of what it claimed to refuse; and the check that pairs the Latin and
English of the Mass only counted lines. A dozen figures in the README and these notes were stale.

### Known limitations

- **Nothing in this release has run on a physical phone.** The update behaviour in particular
  depends on how iOS resumes a home-screen app; it was tested in Chromium by imitating that.
- **Updates are still not instant.** GitHub Pages can take ten minutes to hand out an upload, and
  an app left in the background does not check until it is brought forward.
- **"Fetch afresh" only helps from 0.4.0 on.** It cannot repair a copy that has not yet received
  this release. `check.html` works whatever a browser holds.
- **A file edited by hand on GitHub never reaches an installed copy.** Its fingerprint no longer
  matches, so devices keep the copy they have, and `check.html` names the file. Changes go through
  the project and `uv run biblica-stamp`.
- **An older release published over a newer one is offered, not imposed, but the worker does
  install it**, so the next time the app is started afresh it runs the older release. A deliberate
  rollback works; so would an accidental one.
- **The ordinary form is given by responses and first words**, as explained above. The older
  English beside the Latin is not what is said at Mass now.
- **The traditional Mass has no propers**, and its English is the Divinum Officium project's
  translation, with fourteen slips and solemn-Mass rubrics corrected and listed in
  `tools/sources/mass/traditional.json`. Postures at that Mass are custom, not law, and vary.
- **The Mass companion's explanations were written for the app** and fact-checked once against
  the General Instruction and published guides; see the review notes above for what that found.
- **Whether the Gloria and Creed are said is worked out from the calendar** and is not shown for
  the Triduum. Ritual Masses (a wedding, a funeral) follow their own rules, which the app cannot
  know.
- **Repeat rules do not include "every other week" or "every N days"**, and an entry has one rule.
  For two patterns, add the prayer twice under different names.
- **A daily-list entry "in Lent" runs from Ash Wednesday to Holy Saturday**, which is what people
  mean, not the stricter liturgical reckoning that ends Lent on Holy Thursday.
- **The storage figures are the uncompressed size of what is kept**; the phone may report more
  (its own overhead) or less (compression).
- **"Erase everything" leaves the app's files and the saved Bible text in place.** To remove those
  too, delete the app or clear the site's data.
- **The privacy statement describes this app as shipped.** If you change what your copy stores or
  sends, the statement must change with it.
- **I am not a lawyer.** The licence is the standard MIT text; the terms and the privacy statement
  are plain-language documents written to be accurate about this app, not vetted legal
  instruments. The copyright holder is given as "Biblica contributors"; put your own name there
  if you prefer.
- **The notes on saints and "On this day" are verified only for 9 October to 8 February.** The
  other eight months are still from memory.
- Everything listed under 0.3.0 below still applies, except where this release changed it.

## 0.3.0: study, the journal and the day

### What is new

- **Study a passage.** Select a verse or several in the Bible and choose **Study**. The screen
  shows the passage, its Latin, anything of your own that touches it, what the **Fathers of the
  Church** wrote on it, and **cross-references**. Arrows step from verse to verse.
- **The Fathers.** About 25,700 passages from 82 writers, earliest first: Chrysostom (5,300),
  Augustine (3,900), Gregory the Great, Cyril of Alexandria, Bede, Irenaeus, Cyprian, Jerome,
  Ambrose and many more, a third of them by way of Saint Thomas's **Catena Aurea**. They are
  fullest on the Gospels (15,000 passages), the Psalms, Saint Paul, Acts and Job; 68 of the 73
  books have something. Writers who are not saints, or not who they were once thought to be, carry
  a line saying so.
- **The Latin Vulgate** (Clementine). On the study screen always; in the Bible beneath each verse
  if you turn it on in the text settings, the button at the top right of a chapter.
- **Reflections.** In Notes, a reflection is a note on a theme: "On forgiveness and confession",
  "On being created in the image and likeness of God". Give it a title, write, and add as many
  passages as you like, by typing a reference or from the Bible (select verses, choose Note, then
  "Add to a reflection instead"). Each passage shows a marker in the Bible that leads back to the
  reflection. List them by date or by title.
- **A journal.** One entry to a day: a resolution, thanks, where God was at work, something for
  tomorrow, anything else. The **evening examen** now offers to write the three middle ones as you
  go (it can be turned off on its first screen), and what you ask forgiveness for is never
  written down. Read back through it under Notes or Pray.
- **More guided prayers:** mental prayer with the day's Gospel and a resolution, a visit to the
  Blessed Sacrament, and thanksgiving after Communion. These, the examen and the journal can all be
  put on your **daily list**; finishing one ticks it.
- **A page for every day.** Tap the day's banner on Today. It says what is being celebrated and
  who the saint was, **whether Mass is of obligation** and why, what penance is asked, and what
  happened **on this day** in the Church's history: saints' deaths, councils, apparitions,
  martyrdoms, 986 events across every day of the year. Today itself now marks days of obligation
  in the week strip and shows one event from history.
- **Your saints.** Under Pray: add your confirmation saint, name saint, patron, or any saint you
  love, from the calendar or not, with your own notes. Their feasts are pointed out on Today, and
  a novena can be timed to end on the eve.
- Whatever you are writing is **saved as you go**: a moment after you pause, when you leave the
  screen, and when you put the app away.

### Put right

- **The first book you opened on a new device was not saved for offline use**, and "Save the whole
  Bible" could miss it. The service worker was not yet in charge when it was fetched. The app now
  goes back for anything read before then, and Settings reports what is actually saved, not what
  happens to be in memory. This had been so since 0.1.
- **An update could install with some of the previous version's files.** If a release was
  published within minutes of your last visit, the browser's own cache could hand the new service
  worker old files, leaving the app half one version and half the other until the release after.
  The files are now fetched afresh. I found this by installing 0.2.1 in a test browser and
  publishing 0.3.0 over it, which is now a standing check.
- **About forty Mass readings began or ended a verse out.** Matthew 14:22-36 began at verse 21;
  Hebrews 13:1-8 began with the last verse of chapter 12. The way verse numbers are matched between
  Bibles has been improved, and the lectionary rebuilt with it.
- **A solemnity on a Friday of Lent was called a day of abstinence.** Saint Joseph on 19 March 2027
  is not one.
- Opening a passage of several verses from another screen now marks all of them, not the first.

### How this release was checked, and what that found

I want to be plain about this, because the checking changed the release.

After I had built it and my own tests passed, I had the work reviewed twice by a second pass that
had not seen it being made. The first review found two things that would have damaged your trust
in the app had they shipped:

- **About 1,270 passages of the Fathers sat on the wrong verse**, most of them in the Psalms. The
  source numbers verses as the King James Bible does, and I had run it through a conversion meant
  for a different numbering. Augustine on "Have mercy on me, O God" was under the psalm's title.
- **Text that was not public domain was getting through.** My first rule trusted the source's
  links. Thousands of passages link to an old edition and quote a modern one, several works marked
  their machine translation only on their last line, and one "old" source was a translation of
  1958.

Both are fixed at the root. Placement now goes through the King James text itself, and it was
then read verse by verse in about 190 places where the numberings differ. The selection no longer believes any link: a
passage ships only if its words are found, all but verbatim, in a work on a list of the old
translations that I drew up by hand, or if it is from the Catena Aurea. That cost about 6,000
passages and I think it was plainly right. The second review sampled the result and found nothing
modern or machine-made in it, and found smaller things that are also fixed.

In numbers: 65 logic tests, 160 pipeline tests, and 330 checks in a real browser at phone size
(190 new ones for this release), run against both the folder copy and the flat copy, including an
upgrade from a 0.2 database, a restore, offline use with the network truly cut, and the update
from an installed 0.2.1.

**What was not checked, and should be:**

- **"On this day" and the accounts of the saints are from memory.** I wrote 986 dated events and
  235 short accounts without a book open, then had them checked for consistency, which corrected
  nineteen. I could not check them against sources: I used up this session's allowance of web
  searches on research earlier in the round. The app says this on the screen. If you ask in a new
  message, checking them against sources is the first thing I would do.
- **The list of old translations rests on my knowledge** of what the nineteenth-century series
  contain. Nobody has compared it with the printed volumes.
- **No phone has run this.** Everything was driven in Chromium on a computer.

### Known limitations

- **"On this day" and the accounts of the saints have not been checked against sources** (see
  above). They are labelled, and they are the first thing to verify next.
- **A passage of the Fathers can sit a verse away from the one it is about.** Where the two Bibles
  divide verses differently the match is made by the words, and words can mislead; where the
  source itself filed a passage by an older numbering, only the Psalms and Job are corrected. In a
  check of 147 passages in the places most likely to go wrong, one was out.
- **The Mass readings from Tobit, Judith and Sirach can still be a verse or two out**, as before:
  the Douay-Rheims follows the Latin there and differs from the Greek the lectionary cites.
- **The commentary is what could be verified, not all there is.** Jerome on the prophets, Origen's
  homilies, Ambrose on Luke and much else exist in the source only as recent machine translations
  and are left out. So are about 2,000 passages whose wording a later hand had lightly
  modernised. Whether to carry machine translations, marked as such, is a decision for you.
- **About one passage in a hundred is not letter for letter the old edition**: a "Your" for "Thy",
  or a Scripture reference added in square brackets.
- **Not every writer is a saint.** Origen, Tertullian, Eusebius, Clement of Alexandria and others
  are marked. Ignatius's letters include a longer, later version the old edition printed beside
  the genuine one. Read the Fathers as the Church reads them: together, and under her teaching.
- **Days of obligation are those of the United States**, with the Ascension on Sunday unless you
  change it in Settings. Hawaii follows different rules, and a bishop can dispense; the app knows
  neither. Elsewhere the app states only what is universal.
- **The Latin can be a clause out.** Where the Latin source ends a verse a few words earlier or
  later than the Douay-Rheims, I corrected the thirty-five places a comparison of lengths found.
  Short clauses may remain under the neighbouring verse.
- **The Fathers' files are large**: 28 MB in all, 6 MB for John. They arrive book by book as you
  study, or all at once from Settings. On a phone short of space the app now says if a save failed.
- **No search within the Fathers**, and no way yet to read one Father straight through a book.
- **No lives of the saints at length, and no library of documents** (the Didache, the Confessions,
  the Catechism of Trent). Both are in the roadmap. Haydock's commentary was not found in a form
  that could be used.
- **Cross-references are a general Protestant-compiled set** (OpenBible, from the Treasury of
  Scripture Knowledge). They are useful and are only pointers, but they do not know the
  deuterocanonical books, and their choices are not a Catholic editor's.
- **The journal has no passcode**, like the confession notes, and unlike them it is included in
  backups. Keep the backup file somewhere you trust.
- **A reflection's passages are whole verses**, and a reflection cannot yet be linked to another.
- **Mental prayer's timer is silent**, as the Lectio timer is.
- **Tested in Chromium only.** Safari differs in how it saves when an app is put away, which is
  exactly what item 5 above is for.
- Everything listed under 0.2.0 and 0.1.0 below still applies, except where "Put right" says

  domain and could be added.
- **The examination of conscience comes in one form**, by the Commandments. Forms by state of life
  are on the roadmap.
- **Looking back shows one month at a time**, and only what was ticked. Praying something without
  ticking it leaves no trace, by design.
- **The Lectio timer is silent.** It fills a bar and softens the Next button. It only runs while the

- **Restoring a 0.1 backup empties the daily list, novenas and intentions**, because a restore
  replaces everything and that backup has none.
- **Tested in Chromium only**, as before. Screen wake lock, the share sheet for backups and focus
  behaviour all differ in Safari. The hand tests above are where that is checked.
- Everything listed under 0.1.0 below still applies.

## 0.1.0: the reader

First release. Everything is new.

### What to test by hand

The automated tests cover the calendar, the lectionary and the data. These need a person and a phone:

1. Install to the home screen on your iPhone and confirm it opens full screen.
2. In Settings, save the whole Bible for offline use; then switch on aeroplane mode, close the app
   fully and reopen it. Read a chapter and run a search.
3. Make a note, a highlight and a link. Back up, then restore that backup, and confirm they return.
   On an iPhone the backup goes through the share sheet: check you can save it to Files.
4. Select several verses and check the action bar does not cover what you are selecting.
5. Rotate the phone on the reader.
6. Leave the app open past midnight and check Today moves on when you come back to it.
7. Compare a week of readings against your missal or the USCCB site, including a psalm.

### Known limitations

- **Readings after 2027.** Complete and checked for 2026 and 2027. From January 2028 the Sundays of
  Year C are missing (36 days in 2028), and a few weekdays each year after that lack a first reading.
  Those days say the readings are not in the app yet. This needs a data update before 2028.
- **Verse boundaries in a few books.** Citations are matched to the Douay-Rheims automatically. In
  books where the Vulgate differs from modern editions (Sirach, Tobit, Judith, parts of Wisdom) a
  reading can begin or end a verse out.
- **Half-verses.** A citation such as "Isaiah 35:1-6a" shows the whole of verse 6.
- **The responsorial psalm** is shown as verses, without its refrain.
- **Not the Mass translation.** The Douay-Rheims is not what is read at Mass.
- **Christmas and the Easter Vigil.** The four Masses of Christmas and the Vigil readings come from
  the Latin lectionary's citations, so verse boundaries can differ slightly from the United States
  lectionary. Other vigil Masses (Pentecost, Assumption and so on) are not offered yet.
- **Ascension Thursday.** Where the Ascension is kept on Thursday, turn on the setting; otherwise
  Thursday of the sixth week of Easter shows that weekday's readings.
- **The text is not perfect.** It is the best of two imperfect public domain editions. Report any
  slip you find; the fix belongs in `tools/src/biblica_tools/text_build.py` so it survives rebuilds.
- **Psalm titles** are printed as ordinary verses, as in the source.
- **No paragraphs or poetry layout.** The source has verses only.
- **Holy days of obligation** are shown for the United States only.
- **Local saints.** Only the General Roman Calendar and the United States national calendar are
  known. Diocesan feasts and patronal days are not.
- **Search** loads the whole Bible into memory on first use, which takes a moment on a phone.
- **Tested in Chromium only.** Every screen was exercised in a headless Chromium at phone size,
  online and offline. It has not been run on a real iPhone or in Safari; the hand tests above are
  where that gets checked.
- **Backups replace, they do not merge.** Restoring overwrites what is on the device.
